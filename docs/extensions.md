# Possible extensions of the UNCAWE paper

## Starting point

The paper [Uncertainty-Aware Classifier Accuracy Prediction under Prior Probability Shift](papers/02__Uncertainty_Aware_Classifier_Accuracy_Prediction______QCDS_2026_.pdf) proposes SQS-H, SQS-S, and BayCAP for estimating both the performance of a classifier on unlabelled target data and the uncertainty of that estimate. The current evaluation focuses mainly on vanilla accuracy, tabular datasets, and artificially generated prior probability shift (PPS).

An extended version should go beyond increasing the number of datasets and address at least one new scientific question. The directions below may be combined, although they differ in ambition and cost.

## 1. [[ext1_metrics|Metrics beyond vanilla accuracy]]

A cross-cutting evaluation axis rather than a standalone contribution. It fixes the metric protocol shared by all other extensions: vanilla accuracy (Acc), macro-F1 (F1 of the positive class on binary data, reported separately), and macro-K (macro-averaged informedness), with the paper's table convention $T=C\operatorname{diag}(\pi)$, the sample mean $\hat A=\frac1m\sum_r A(T^{(r)})$ as point estimate, and explicit edge-case conventions for rare or absent classes.

Its own analysis is analysis-only on existing `main` results: per-metric point and interval quality, the gap $\lvert A(\bar T)-\hat A\rvert$, asymmetry of the metric distributions, and behaviour on bags with rare or absent classes. It also tests a new hypothesis: under PPS, Acc is linear in $\pi$, macro-F1 is nonlinear in $\pi$, and macro-K depends on $\pi$ only weakly (not at all for $K=2$), so comparing interval quality across metrics separates prevalence-estimation error from error in $C$.

The full protocol and analyses are described in [ext1_metrics.md](ext1_metrics.md). No new runs are required.

## 2. [[ext2_baycap|Does BayCAP describe the right accuracy?]]

BayCAP's interval describes the population table $C\operatorname{diag}(\pi^Q)$, i.e. the accuracy the classifier has on average over bags with a given prevalence, while the paper scores it against the realised table of the target bag $U$. On a finite bag the two differ, because the classifier is right on a random number of instances. The extension asks whether this mismatch makes BayCAP's intervals miscalibrated on the paper's datasets and, if so, fixes it with the realised-table posterior predictive: for each posterior draw, it samples the true classes behind the observed target predictions and applies the metric to the resulting table of $U$, with no target labels and no tuning. In a binary toy simulation, the population interval covers the realised accuracy only $63\%$ of the time when $V$ has $5000$ instances per class, and the predictive restores near-nominal coverage.

The document is experiment-driven. E2.1 approximates the predictive for accuracy on stored `main` results (`tables/estimand_gap.py`): BayCAP under-covers on $53$ of $75$ (dataset, classifier) pairs ($51$ after FIX-8), but the predictive repairs only $5$, because on many pairs, and on every pair of `isolet` and `letter`, the interval is displaced rather than too narrow. E2.2, at no run cost, studies three candidate causes of the displacement on stored results: the uniform prior on the columns of $C$, which pulls the recalls towards $1/K$; an ill-conditioned $C$, which amplifies errors into the estimated prevalences; and the difficulty of the problem on i.i.d. data. It splits BayCAP's error into a recall term and a prevalence term, and reports per pair the conditioning of $C$ (condition number $\kappa_0$ and absolute amplification $1/\sigma_{\min}$ on vectors summing to zero; $\kappa_0$ is always $1$ when $K=2$) and the accuracy of the classifier on $V$. Its run (2026-10-20, generated again on the CPU results of `BayesCAP` on 2026-10-22) finds that coverage is almost entirely a matter of displacement: the prior on $C$ accounts for every pair with coverage near $0$ (large $K$, few validation instances per class, recall term equal to its prior part); a badly conditioned $C$ displaces the interval only when the prevalence error has the same sign in every bag (`german`, `wine-quality`), and otherwise just widens it; a low accuracy on $V$ does not act on its own; on binary datasets a smaller recall error remains that the prior does not explain, possibly a difference between $V$ and the test pool shared by all bags (cause 4, a new hypothesis, recorded as a guess and not tested). E2.3 runs the exact predictive in `main` as the method `BayesCAP-real` (`BayCAP` with `estimand="realised"`, excluded by default from the paper's tables) and compares it with the population interval: the realised interval is wider by exactly the bag-level spread, $\sqrt{1+(\hat\sigma_g/\hat\sigma_p)^2}$ times, and its centre does not move (within $0.009$), so the causes found by E2.2 are unchanged; coverage never drops, $16$ pairs reach $0.95$ (from $12$ to $28$ pairs at or above $0.95$), and a paired bootstrap finds the Winkler score significantly lower on $31$ pairs, all displaced ones, and slightly higher on $9$ pairs that already covered; the approximation of E2.1 is accurate within about $0.01$ of coverage. E2.4 replaces the uniform prior on the columns of $C$ with an empirical-Bayes prior: the recalls of a classifier get a common Beta prior whose centre (the typical recall) and strength are fitted to the validation counts by maximum likelihood of the beta-binomial, so each recall is pulled towards the typical recall of the classifier instead of towards $1/K$; only the concentration parameters of the Dirichlet prior change, so BayesianCC is otherwise unchanged. It runs the new prior with both pushforwards (`BayesCAP-EB`, `BayesCAP-EB-real`) against `BayesCAP` and `BayesCAP-real`, predicts its effect on the recall term in closed form from $V$ alone (a zero-cost check), and stores the NUTS diagnostics of all four methods. Its run (2026-10-26) finds that the check predicts the change of the recall term almost exactly (correlation $0.9995$); the large-$K$ displacement disappears (`isolet` and `letter` go from coverage $0$ to $0.84$–$0.96$ with the realised pushforward), and with the realised pushforward the pairs with coverage at least $0.90$ go from $34$ to $52$ and the mean Winkler score from $0.84$ to $0.32$. Two problems remain: on binary datasets the fit has two counts to estimate a spread, its strength can reach the upper bound and make the interval too narrow (`mammographic`), and no binary pair gains coverage; and with many classes and accurate classifiers the off-diagonal concentrations fall below $1$, NUTS diverges in most bags on $18$ pairs, and the gains there (all of `isolet`, most of `poker_hand`) cannot yet be trusted. Two toy simulations explain the binary failures (§T.10, §T.13 and Appendix A of the extension): with $K=2$ the strength of the prior is decided by a single difference between two recalls, so it often reaches the bound even when the recalls differ, and the plug-in prior then drops the uncertainty of its centre, which lowers the coverage even when the two recalls are truly equal; with a moderate strength the prior pulls the true-positive and true-negative rates together, although they differ for a structural reason; and at $K=2$ the uniform prior was already nearly calibrated. On `mammographic` the failure adds to cause 4: the bags are re-drawn with replacement from a test pool of $249$ instances, whose own sampling error the interval does not include, and in the toy this alone lowers the coverage of the uniform prior from $0.96$ to $0.85$. Concentrations below $1$ put most of the prior mass of the unobserved cells against the faces of the simplex, which NUTS cannot explore with the step size that suits the observed cells (§T.11). E2.5 repeats E2.4 on the $25$ binary datasets of `quapy` that `main` does not use, in an experiment separate from `main`. Its run (2026-10-28/29) finds the binary failure general and predicted by the gap statistic $z$ of the two recalls of $V$: the strength reaches its bound on all $11$ pairs with $\lvert z\rvert<1$, on $11$ of $35$ with $1\le\lvert z\rvert<2$ and on $2$ of $74$ with $\lvert z\rvert\ge2$, and the realised coverage falls by $0.35$ on average when $\lvert z\rvert<1$, while with $\lvert z\rvert\ge2$ the prior is about neutral. With a finite strength the predicted pull is small, because the fitted strength falls as the gap grows and the pull cancels over bags; the harm comes from a narrower interval around a centre already displaced. The EB prior helps only with high recalls and few validation instances per class, where the uniform pull is large. The uniform prior itself covers $0.85$ on pools of at least $100$ instances and $0.67$ on smaller ones, with a recall error that its prior does not explain, consistent with cause 4 (not studied there). NUTS diverges on small datasets with both priors, and with the EB prior whenever a Beta parameter is below $1$. Cause 4 is studied for all methods in Extension 4 (2026-10-29). The user decided on 2026-10-29 that BayCAP may depart from BayesianCC and be presented as a Bayesian CAP method of its own; E2.6 screens at no cost, on the pairs of `main`, a hierarchical prior on the recalls, which puts a prior on the typical recall $m$ and the strength $\nu$ instead of plugging in their estimates: it predicts the method's intervals from $V$ alone, after validating that prediction on the stored empirical-Bayes results and diagnosing its misses, and measures the sensitivity to the prior on $\nu$, whether the classes of a classifier are alike enough to be pooled, and the risk of the sampler problems of the empirical-Bayes prior. Its run (2026-10-29) finds that the answer barely depends on the prior on $\nu$ (median range of the predicted coverage $0$) and that no class is atypical enough to be displaced by pooling; the prediction from $V$ gets the change of the centre right where the estimated prevalences do not move, but not the change of the spread, so it is within $0.05$ of the stored empirical-Bayes coverage on only $0.69$ of the pairs ($0.44$ at $K\ge8$); the divergences of the empirical-Bayes prior follow the parameters of the posterior, not of the prior, and for the hierarchical model they point to two separate risks, a class perfect or never right on $V$ ($22$ pairs at high risk) and nearly complete pooling, the funnel ($12$ pairs). E2.7 runs BayCAP with the hierarchical prior (`BayesCAP-H-real`: $m$ and $u=1/\sqrt{1+\nu}$ uniform, the NUTS sampler of BayCAP unchanged, realised pushforward) on the pairs of `main`. Its run finds that it keeps the large-$K$ gains of the empirical-Bayes prior without its binary collapse (mean realised Winkler score $0.31$, against $0.32$ for the empirical-Bayes prior and $0.84$ for the uniform one; $0.29$ at $K=2$, against $0.53$ and $0.28$); it loses to the empirical-Bayes prior only on `wine-quality` and `poker_hand`, where that prior gains through the shares of the mistakes, which the hierarchical prior keeps uniform (a new hypothesis). The prediction from $V$ is as good as for the empirical-Bayes prior, and the variance of the estimated prevalences explains its misses only where $C$ is badly conditioned. NUTS does not mix on $9$ pairs, mostly where $V$ says little about $\nu$ (a funnel: `mammographic`, `hand_digits`), while classes perfect on $V$ are mostly harmless. E2.8 runs BayCAP-Hf, BayCAP-H with two fixes: a uniform prior on $\rho=1/(1+\nu)$, the share of the largest variance that the recalls have, and a concentration $\kappa$ of the shares of the mistakes learned from the off-diagonal counts of $V$, with $1/(1+\kappa)$ uniform. The lighter tail removes the funnel where the prior caused it (the binary pairs, `mammographic`) but not on `hand_digits`, where the data push $\nu$ up; the learned $\kappa$ is below $1$ on $47$ of the $50$ multiclass pairs, halves the prevalence term of the shift and closes the gap to the empirical-Bayes prior on `wine-quality` and `poker_hand` (mean realised Winkler score $0.23$, against $0.31$ for BayCAP-H); BayCAP-Hf becomes the candidate method on the $52$ pairs where NUTS is reliable, but with the centred Dirichlet the sampler diverges on $18$ pairs (all of `poker_hand` and `isolet` among them). E2.9 runs the same model with the shares in a non-centred form, $\ln g_{ij}=\ln G'_{ij}-e_{ij}/\kappa$ with $G'_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\kappa+1,1)$ and $e_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)$, normalised, an exact identity that moves $\kappa$ out of the law of the sampled variables, judged on the sampler alone (the funnel of $\nu$ left as it is). A check on the stored E2.8 results finds the divergences of BayCAP-Hf following the share of empty cells of $V$ (Spearman $0.72$) and not the observed mistakes per column ($-0.29$), and the pilot (2026-11-03) makes the sampler worse on all three boundary pairs it contains (divergent draws $5$–$65\%\to46$–$99.8\%$ on `nursery` LR, `poker_hand` LR, `isolet` kNN) while the control `letter` kNN stays clean with the same posterior; the full run was not done, and BayCAP-Hf stays the candidate method, with its unreliable pairs flagged. A possible reason, not tested: the non-centred form helps the empty cells but builds a new funnel in the cells that $V$ informs. A diagnosis against the size of $V$ (E2.c1), a factorial separating the estimand gap from the fixed class counts (E2.c2) and a reference run of BayCAP-Hf on its $18$ boundary pairs and on `letter` (E2.c7: a smaller step size, a longer warm-up and four chains on $10$ bags per pair, to measure whether the divergences and the slow mixing of the centred sampler change the accuracy interval, since its median split-$\hat R$ stays low while up to $65\%$ of the draws are divergent) are further candidates. Tempering and other priors are in Extension 5.

The full description is in [ext2_baycap.md](ext2_baycap.md).

## 3. [[ext3_posterior_propagation|Propagating a Bayesian prevalence posterior to classifier performance]]

Merges the former Extensions 8 (SQS-B) and 9 (BayCAP-KDEy) (2026-10-27). The extension adds CAP methods that turn the posterior of a Bayesian quantifier over $\pi^Q$ into an interval on performance, and compares them with the methods of the paper. BayCAP with a fixed $\hat C$ pushes each joint posterior draw $\pi^{(r)}$ through the rate matrix of $h$ measured on $V$, $\hat C\operatorname{diag}(\pi^{(r)})$, either as a population table or, with the realised-table predictive of Extension 2, as a plausible realised table of the target bag. Two quantifiers are considered (2026-10-28): the Bayesian KDEy of [kde_bayes.pdf](papers/kde_bayes.pdf) (BayesKDEy, giving BayCAP-KDEy), which estimates from $V$ the densities of the outputs of an input classifier within each class, and QuaPy's `BayesianMAPLS`, a Bayesian EM quantifier (giving BayCAP-EMQ), which reads the outputs as calibrated probabilities; the input classifier is $h$ itself or a separate MLP trained on $V$. SQS-B does what the other SQS methods do, but draws the prevalences of its validation bags (one bag per joint draw) from the posterior of a Bayesian quantifier instead of from a point estimate or a bootstrap. Each method differs from an existing one in a single component, so the comparison says which component matters; Extension 4 measures the error split of the realised BayCAP-KDEy with an MLP input, with the implementation of E3.1.

The theory derives that, with a fixed $\hat C$, accuracy is $\hat d^\top\pi$, so the population interval has variance $(\hat d-\bar d\mathbf 1)^\top\Sigma_\pi(\hat d-\bar d\mathbf 1)$ and is narrow when the recalls are similar; that it leaves out the luck of the bag; that the realised pushforward should widen it by $\sqrt{1+(\bar\sigma_g/\sigma_p)^2}$ and, unlike in BayCAP, may move its centre, because neither quantifier fits $\pi^Q$ to the predictions of $h$ (less so when it reads $h$); that $\hat C$ has no prior, so the pull towards $1/K$ that displaces BayCAP on large-$K$ datasets is absent; that the EM likelihood has the same mixture form as the KDEy one, with the densities within each class taken from the calibration of the input classifier; and that QuaPy's `BayesianMAPLS` has a logistic-normal prior, which vanishes at the edges of the simplex (new hypotheses where not derived). E3.1 (card WP-8, run on 2026-11-05) crosses quantifier ($\{$BayesKDEy, BayesianMAPLS$\}$), input classifier ($\{h$, MLP$\}$) and pushforward (population, realised) on the protocol of `main`: eight methods, with BayesKDEy at the Gaussian kernel, $b=0.1$, $\lambda=0$, BayesianMAPLS as QuaPy has it, and $\tau=1$. Results (E3.1): the population interval under-covers (mean coverage $0.26$–$0.32$ after FIX-8, $0.25$–$0.33$ before); the realised pushforward widens it by the predicted factor (median $3$–$3.4$, within $10\%$ of the prediction on $61$–$70$ of $75$ pairs) and the centre barely moves; without a prior on $C$ the displacement of BayCAP on the large-$K$ datasets is gone; the realised methods represent only $e_1^U$ and $e_3$ and fail where the error comes from $V$ (the rates of $h$ on `mammographic` and `semeion`; on `poker_hand`, after FIX-8, a prevalence error shared by the pairs of the dataset, with coverage $0.08$–$0.25$ with the MLP input) or the quantifiers share a bias (`wine-quality`); BayesKDEy beats BayesianMAPLS, which is hurt by miscalibrated inputs, while the prior of BayesianMAPLS has no visible effect (a test confounded with $K$) and the input classifier matters little for BayesKDEy on most pairs (before FIX-8 an MLP helped when $h$ is LR; after FIX-8 that rested on `poker_hand`, where the MLP input now fails, so $h$ is the better input by the mean Winkler score and the MLP by the mean rank); BayCAP-KDEy with the realised pushforward is the best of the eight methods, with narrower intervals than BayCAP, the best AE rank and a mean Winkler rank level with BayCAP-Hf, but a lower coverage and a higher mean Winkler score than BayCAP-Hf, and only the realised methods are considered from now on. E3.2 (the former candidate E3.c5, card WP-13, run on 2026-11-07) makes the rate matrix of the realised BayCAP-KDEy random: each class of $V$ is split into cells by the prediction of $h$, the kernel density of the class becomes a mixture of cell densities with the column of $C$ as weights, and $(\pi,C)$ is sampled jointly by NUTS with the Hf prior of Extension 2 and the validation counts as in BayCAP, so that the target bag updates $C$ through the outputs of the input classifier; with $h$ the likelihood uses the outputs alone, with the MLP the outputs and the predictions of $h$; it is compared with the realised BayCAP-KDEy of E3.1 (fixed $\hat C$) and with BayCAP-Hf (counts of the predictions instead of kernel densities), and it needed the split of `BayCAPFixed` into `BayCAPKDEy` and `BayCAPMAPLS`. Results (E3.2): no improvement over E3.1 overall (after FIX-8, mean Winkler score $0.44$ and $0.47$ against $0.47$ and $0.51$, lower only through `poker_hand`; before, $0.41$ and $0.40$ against $0.43$ and $0.39$; the better Winkler rank of the MLP variant comes from many negligible wins and is not significant, Wilcoxon $p=0.45$, $0.91$ before); it helps where the error is in the rates of $h$ (`mammographic`, `semeion`), improves `poker_hand` only in part and only with the MLP, leaves `wine-quality` unchanged with the MLP, fails on `shuttle` (MLP) and `german` ($h$), and makes the predicted shares match those of the bag; a likely reason (new hypothesis) is that the cell densities, fixed at their estimates on $V$, identify $\pi$ whatever $C$ is. By the user's decision of 2026-11-09 the methods of E3.2 are not carried forward: BayCAP-KDEy with the MLP and a fixed $\hat C$ stays the BayCAP-KDEy method (most accurate centre, narrowest intervals, best on most pairs) and BayCAP-Hf the reference for coverage and mean Winkler score, which it wins on the few datasets where the error comes from $V$. E3.3 (the former candidate E3.c1, decided on 2026-11-10, not run) evaluates SQS-B with BayesKDEy on the MLP: one bag of $V$ per posterior draw, drawn as in `SQS-H(bs)`; with the draws of the realised BayCAP-KDEy with the MLP, it changes only the bridge from the prevalence to the table (a bag simulated forwards from $V$ instead of true classes sampled backwards given the predictions of $h$), so the theory predicts the centre of the population method and a width close to that of the realised one (their bag terms differ by the spread of the recalls against that of the precisions); it is compared with `BayCAP-KDEy-mlp-real`, `SQS-H`, `SQS-Hbs` and `BayesCAP-Hf-real`, added to the tables of `main`, and depends on FIX-8 and on the re-run of `main` of FIX-2 + FIX-3 (both done); the MLP input is kept after FIX-8, although it fails on `poker_hand` in E3.1, which E3.3 reports (user's decision of 2026-11-13). Candidates: BayCAP with a fixed $\hat C$ (E3.c2), a random rate matrix at the bridge (E3.c3) or through a cut posterior on Bayesian-bootstrap weights of $V$ (E3.c4, new hypothesis), BayesianMAPLS with a uniform prior (E3.c6) and with a random calibration map sampled jointly with $\pi$ (E3.c7, new hypothesis), a blend of BayCAP-KDEy and BayCAP-Hf driven by a label-free signal that $V$ is unreliable (E3.c8, new hypothesis), and `SQS-Hbs` with KDEyML on an MLP, which separates the quantifier from the source of the prevalences in E3.3 (E3.c9). Time and memory measurements, the generic sensitivity analyses and the other ablations of the former documents are set aside in [[optional_extensions]] §8.

The full description is in [ext3_posterior_propagation.md](ext3_posterior_propagation.md). `BayCAPKDEy` (BayesKDEy) and `BayCAPMAPLS` (BayesianMAPLS), with $h$ or an MLP as input classifier and the population or realised pushforward (one class, `BayCAPFixed`, until WP-13 split it with the same draws), and the analysis of E3.1 are implemented, and E3.1 is run and analysed (card WP-8, done); E3.2 (`BayCAPKDEy` with `c_prior="hierarchical-f"`), its tables and plots are implemented, and E3.2 is run and analysed (card WP-13, done); SQS-B (E3.3, `methods/sqs_b.py:SQSB`), its tables and plots are implemented, and E3.3 is run (2026-11-13, without a pilot) and analysed (card WP-18): SQS-B has the centre of the population method and about the width of `BayCAP-KDEy-mlp-real`, but is worse than it (lower Winkler score on $2$ pairs, higher on $24$), because its forward bag ignores the predictions of $h$ on the target bag; it ties with `SQS-Hbs` on vanilla accuracy and beats it on macro-F1 and macro-K.

## 4. [[ext4_uncertainty_budget|The error split of CAP under PPS]]

Narrowed by the user on 2026-11-09 from "The uncertainty budget of CAP under PPS" to the error split itself, its behaviour across the sizes of the target bag and of $V$, and oracle substitutions of its components. The error $\hat A-A(h,U)$ of every CAP method that estimates a contingency table $\hat T$ (prevalences $\tilde\pi_j=\sum_i\hat T_{ij}$, recalls $\tilde d_j=\hat T_{jj}/\tilde\pi_j$) splits exactly into a prevalence part $e_1=\sum_j(\tilde\pi_j-\pi^Q_j)\tilde d_j$, a recall part $e_2=\sum_j\pi^Q_j(\tilde d_j-d^{\text{pool}}_j)$ and the luck of the bag $e_3=\sum_j\pi^Q_j(d^{\text{pool}}_j-c_j/n_j)$, scored against the realised table of each bag; the recalls of $h$ on the test pool stand for its population recalls (user's decision of 2026-11-09), so the error of the pool is merged into $e_2$. The extension is descriptive: it measures the parts and does not model their variances. It considers the methods of the main table (`tables/main.py:MAIN_METHODS`) that estimate a table: `QuAcc_NxN`, `O-LEAP`, SQS-H (`RQBS`), SQS-S (`PQ`), and BayCAP, BayCAP-Hf and BayCAP-KDEy (MLP input, fixed $\hat C$) with the realised-table predictive; `DoC` estimates accuracy without a table and is left out. Experiments: E4.1 (run and analysed on 2026-11-10, card WP-14) measures the three parts on the stored results of `main` at $\lvert U\rvert=1000$, to see which one drives the error of each method: the recalls on the binary datasets, with an offset shared by every method that is ascribed, as a hypothesis, to the finite $V$, and the prevalences on the multiclass ones; E4.2 (E4.2b run and analysed on 2026-11-15, card WP-21; E4.2a run and analysed on 2026-11-15, card WP-22) measures them for BayCAP-Hf and BayCAP-KDEy, splitting each part within a cell of equal prevalence, size and $V$ into a part shared by the bags (driven by $V$) and a part that changes from bag to bag (driven by $U$), in two parts that vary one size at a time (2026-11-11): E4.2a over $\lvert U\rvert\in\{100,\dots,10000\}$ with the full $V$, E4.2b over nested stratified subsamples of $V$, with several seeds per level, at $\lvert U\rvert=1000$, which tests the finite-$V$ hypothesis of E4.1 and supports it in the form "finite $V$ and finite pool" (the gap between the recalls of $h$ on $V$ and on the pool is within $1.7$ standard deviations of two independent samples), finds that the fixed part of $e_2$ grows as $V$ shrinks for both methods and becomes a systematic shift when the smallest class keeps a few tens of instances, and that the realised-table predictive makes the recalls of BayCAP-KDEy depend on the bag; E4.2a finds that every bag part, the bag part of $e_2$ of BayCAP-KDEy included, shrinks as $1/\sqrt{\lvert U\rvert}$ while the fixed parts barely move, so that from $\lvert U\rvert=1000$ on the error is mostly an offset shared by the bags, with exceptions where the prevalence error itself changes with the bag (`letter`, `wine-quality`); the interaction of the two sizes is candidate E4.c13; E4.3 (run and analysed on 2026-11-10, card WP-14) replaces, after estimation, the prevalences with the true ones and the recalls with the pool ones in a $2\times2$ design on stored results, whose interaction is the cross term $\sum_j(\tilde\pi_j-\pi^Q_j)(\tilde d_j-d^{\text{pool}}_j)$. The theory also bounds the parts (brought back on 2026-11-09 from the earlier version): $\lvert e_1\rvert\le\frac12\lVert\tilde\pi-\pi^Q\rVert_1(\max_j\tilde d_j-\min_j\tilde d_j)$, an equality when $K=2$, the cross term by the same lemma with the recall errors in place of the recalls, and $e_2$, $e_3$ between the smallest and largest of their per-class errors. Oracles fed into the methods, which would also give a coverage, are candidate E4.c11; the tightness of the bounds on stored results is candidate E4.c12. The resolution of FIX-1 ([[work_plan]] §III.3, card FIX-1 + FIX-4: the fix pinned from `cap-ml` and `PQ` re-run) is a gate for every experiment (user's decision of 2026-11-09), met on 2026-11-09. The earlier material (the first-order variance model of the parts, the coverage rule and regime map, width floors, label-free plug-ins, completed and guaranteed intervals, the parts of BayCAP with joint estimation, the pool term and the protocol without replacement, and the toy simulation of the budget) is set aside in [[optional_extensions]] §10.

## 5. [[ext5_baycap_priors_tempering|Prevalence priors and tempering for BayCAP-Hf and BayCAP-KDEy]]

The paper names informative priors and posterior tempering as unexplored features of BayCAP. Restructured on 2026-11-10 and kept thin (user's decision), the extension studies them only for the two Bayesian methods kept by Extensions 2 and 3, BayCAP-Hf (`BayesCAP-Hf-real`) and BayCAP-KDEy with an MLP input and a fixed $\hat C$ (`BayCAP-KDEy-mlp-real`), both with the realised pushforward and scored against the realised table of each bag. E5.1 measures how much the knowledge of the distribution $G$ of the target prevalences helps: on the $75$ pairs of `main`, with bags of $100$ and $1000$ instances drawn at prevalences from $G=\operatorname{Dir}(3K\mu^\star)$, whose concentration grows with $K$ so that $G$ is about three times less spread than the uniform distribution on every dataset, and whose mean $\mu^\star$ is a seeded point of the simplex away from its centre, it compares the uniform prior on $\pi^Q$ with the well-specified prior $G$ and with a misspecified prior with the same concentration and another seeded mean; under the protocol of `main` the uniform prior is already the well-specified one. Its run (2026-11-14) finds that the well-specified prior lowers the mean Winkler score of both methods at both bag sizes ($0.237\to0.190$ for BayCAP-Hf at $\lvert U\rvert=100$), mostly by recentring the interval (amplitudes $1$–$8\%$ narrower), but the gain is concentrated on `connect-4`, `poker_hand` and `wine-quality`, where the uniform prior undercovers because of an error in the prevalence; on a typical dataset it is close to zero, and over the $15$ datasets it is not significant. The misspecified prior costs several times more than the right one gains ($0.237\to0.636$), on largely the same datasets. Contrary to the theory, the gain does not grow with $K$, and it falls on pairs that undercover rather than on pairs that already cover; where the error is in the recalls (`mammographic`) the prior does not help. E5.2 measures how much tempering helps: on the protocol of `main`, $\tau\in\{0.5,1,2,4,8\}$, BayCAP-Hf tempered jointly on its validation and bag terms, BayCAP-KDEy on its bag term; the tempered method takes the $\tau$ chosen on the labelled bags of the other $14$ datasets (leave one dataset out: no label of the target dataset, but the labels of a benchmark, so it assumes that the best $\tau$ carries over to a new dataset and that the shift in use resembles the benchmark's), and is retained if it beats $\tau=1$ by a paired Wilcoxon test over the $15$ datasets; the per-pair oracle-best $\tau$ is a reference. Its run (2026-11-14/15) retains tempering for neither method: the selection is stable ($\tau_{-d}=2$ for BayCAP-Hf and $8$ for BayCAP-KDEy on every held-out dataset) and lowers the mean Winkler score ($0.237\to0.183$ and $0.508\to0.207$), but only on $7$ of the $15$ datasets for both ($p=0.36$ and $0.21$): the gain comes from three datasets with displaced intervals (`german`, `wine-quality`, `mammographic` for BayCAP-Hf; `poker_hand`, `wine-quality`, `mammographic` for BayCAP-KDEy), while the other datasets, mostly those that already cover, get slightly worse. Tempering widens without moving the centre, as predicted; the widening of BayCAP-KDEy follows $\sqrt{(\tau+r^2)/(1+r^2)}$ (correlation $0.97$ on the log scale) but exceeds it on binary datasets ($1.19$ against $1.05$ at $\tau=4$); with BayCAP-Hf a larger $\tau$ lowers the divergent draws but raises split-$\hat R$ at $K\ge8$. The theory predicts small effects: the prior weighs about $c/(c+N_{\text{eff}}/\tau)$, with $N_{\text{eff}}$ the number of labels that the bag is worth, and neither a prior on $\pi^Q$ nor a wider posterior moves an interval displaced by the error from $V$; for BayCAP-KDEy, tempering widens the realised interval by $\sqrt{(\tau+r^2)/(1+r^2)}$, $r=\bar\sigma_g/\sigma_p$, which the medians of E3.1 put at $1.04$–$1.07$ for $\tau=4$ on binary datasets. E5.3 (the former candidate E5.c2, promoted by the user on 2026-11-15, card WP-23) chooses $\tau$ for each pair without any label outside $V$: $V$ is split into two halves, both methods are refitted on one and run at the five values of $\tau$ on $50$ calibration bags drawn from the other, $\tau_{\text{cal}}$ is the value with the lowest Winkler score on them, and the deployed method, fitted on the full $V$, is read at $\tau_{\text{cal}}$ from the stored results of E5.2; the decision rule is that of E5.2, against $\tau=1$. With the deployed method fitted on the full $V$, the error on the calibration bags is uncorrelated with the error on the test bags, so the rule sets the width from the expected size of the error from $V$ and from systematic biases, not from the actual displacement of a pair, and it is expected to choose too large a $\tau$ (its calibration error has twice the standard deviation). Candidates: tempering one likelihood term of BayCAP-Hf (E5.c1), prior $\times$ temperature (E5.c3). E5.4 (promoted directly by the user on 2026-11-18, card WP-24) asks about another kind of knowledge of the prevalences, how extreme the shift is rather than its direction: on the $75$ pairs and bags of $100$ and $1000$ instances, the prevalences are drawn from a concentrated distribution $G_c=\operatorname{Dir}(3\cdot\mathbf 1)$ (the $G$ of E5.1 with its mean at the centre) or from a sparse one $G_e=\operatorname{Dir}(\tfrac13\mathbf 1)$, near the faces of the simplex, and each set of bags is analysed with the uniform prior, the well-informed prior and the ill-informed one (the other distribution). The concentrated prior is worth $3K$ imaginary instances and the sparse one $K/3$, fewer than the uniform prior, so the theory predicts an asymmetric outcome: a large cost only when the bags are sparse and the prior is concentrated, which lifts the classes absent from the bag towards $1/K$. Its run (2026-11-18/19) confirms the asymmetry: on sparse bags the concentrated prior raises the mean Winkler score of BayCAP-Hf from $0.302$ to $0.536$ at $\lvert U\rvert=100$ and is worse than the uniform prior on all $15$ datasets, while every other cell moves by less than $0.07$. Knowing how extreme the shift is helps little ($\Delta W_{\text{know}}\le0.043$, less than knowing its direction in E5.1), and the wrong belief costs more than the right one gains in all four cells. Contrary to the theory, the cost on the accuracy is at small $K$, not on the many absent classes at $K\ge8$, where similar recalls make the accuracy insensitive to the prevalence; on macro-F1 and macro-K, which weigh every class, both the gain and the cost under the sparse distribution are several times larger. The sparse prior makes BayCAP-KDEy diverge on up to $22\%$ of the sparse bags; BayCAP-Hf diverges as often as with the uniform prior. E5.5 (decided by the user on 2026-11-19, card WP-27) asks whether a prior that knows only the side of the shift still helps: the bags of $1000$ instances come from more extreme distributions, $\operatorname{Dir}(10\cdot\mathbf 1)$ and $\operatorname{Dir}(\tfrac1{10}\mathbf 1)$, and are analysed with the priors of E5.4, so the prior on the right side is less extreme than the truth; SQS-B is run with the three priors, and SQS-H, SQS-Hbs and SQS-S, which take no prior, are references that do not move with the information. In a simplified model the error of the posterior mean grows with the distance of the prior weight from that of the matched prior, so a right-side prior always beats the uniform one on average and a wrong-side prior always loses, most on sparse bags with many classes. Not run.

The full description is in [ext5_baycap_priors_tempering.md](ext5_baycap_priors_tempering.md). Both methods accept a Dirichlet prior on $\pi^Q$ and a temperature (the `temperature` of `BayCAP` since WP-20); E5.1 (option `--e51` of `binform`) and E5.2 (the tempered methods in `main`) are run and analysed, and so is E5.3 (study `tau_select`: $\tau$ chosen per pair on calibration bags drawn from half of $V$), whose selection is not retained, so $\tau=1$ is kept for both methods; E5.4 (option `--e54` of `binform`) is run and analysed; E5.5 is scheduled. The prior on $C$, the comparison of tempering with the realised-table predictive, the full grid of priors on $\pi^Q$ and the earlier hypotheses and paper plan are set aside in [[optional_extensions]] §12.

## 6. [[ext6_sqs_s_design|How SQS-S uses the validation set]]

SQS-S estimates the accuracy on a target bag from labelled bags drawn from $V$ whose estimated prevalence is close to that of the target. Two design choices are studied on the corrected SQS-S of `main` (after FIX-1, which trains the quantifier on $V_q$ only and estimates bags and target with the same pipeline), one experiment each, keeping the extension thin (user's decision of 2026-11-09). E6.1 compares the $L_1$, Hellinger and Jensen–Shannon distances with the `predist` diagnostic, extended to the five classifiers and with the quantifier of `PQ`, PACC(MLP) (user's decision of 2026-11-10); $L_1$ is kept unless another distance is clearly better; run on 2026-11-10: $L_1$ is kept, the three distances perform almost the same, and the selection is limited by the size of the neighbourhood at the edges of the simplex and by the quantifier. E6.2, an analysis without a decision rule (user's decision of 2026-11-10), varies the allocation $\rho=\lvert V_q\rvert/\lvert V\rvert$ over $\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9\}$ on the full `main` protocol, with nested splits and no outer seeds: a larger $\rho$ improves the quantifier, a smaller one improves the recalls of $h$ inside the bags, whose error is shared by all the bags and so causes under-coverage; run on 2026-11-13 on the $75$ pairs: the allocation matters only at the edges, and asymmetrically ($\rho=0.1$ doubles the quantification error but leaves the estimate and the interval of accuracy as at $\rho=0.5$; $\rho=0.9$ makes the interval miss through the shared error, on the datasets with a small $V$), the curve is flat over $0.3$–$0.7$, and $\rho=0.5$ is kept. Candidates: cross-fitting (E6.c1), sensitivity to $\gamma$ (E6.c2), kernel weights (E6.c3).

The full description is in [ext6_sqs_s_design.md](ext6_sqs_s_design.md). SQS-S and `predist` are implemented; the parameter $\rho$ of `PrediQuant` (to be added in `cap-ml`, with the current split kept as the default) and the experiments are not. Adaptive bandwidth, Aitchison geometry, the factorial study of $\rho$, $\lvert V\rvert$ and $\lvert U\rvert$, the Neyman allocation across classes and the label-free selection of $\rho$ are set aside in [[optional_extensions]] §11.

## 7. [[ext7_kdey_bandwidth|Selecting the bandwidth of BayesKDEy]]

Narrowed by the user on 2026-11-19 from a factorial grid-search study of the classifiers and quantifiers to a single question, one experiment at a time and with no candidates for now: does a bandwidth selected from $V$ give `BayCAP-KDEy-mlp-real` better intervals? In `main`, BayesKDEy uses one Gaussian bandwidth $b=0.1$ for every class and dataset; the bandwidth sets how sharp the class densities of the outputs of the input classifier are, and so how narrow the posterior of $\pi^Q$ is and where it is centred. E7.1 (card WP-25, pilot only, closed on 2026-11-19) selected one bandwidth per class by the leave-one-out log-likelihood of the class densities on the cross-fitted validation outputs of the MLP, with the densities normalised in the $K-1$ dimensions of the simplex (with per-class bandwidths QuaPy's $K$-dimensional normalisation would reweight the classes and bias the selection towards small bandwidths). On the pilot (`mammographic`, `wine-quality`, $10$ pairs) it picked bandwidths well below $0.1$ (median $0.025$–$0.035$), which fit the densities better, and both `BayCAP-KDEy-mlp-real` and `SQS-B` got worse on every metric (vanilla accuracy, mean Winkler score $1.92\to3.86$ and $2.31\to3.95$), the centre moving away from the target on `wine-quality`; it is not carried forward. E7.2 (card WP-26, not run) selects one bandwidth per pair, shared by the classes, on calibration bags as E5.3 did for the temperature: $V$ is split in halves, BayesKDEy is fitted on $V_{\text{fit}}$, $50$ bags of size $1000$ are drawn from $V_{\text{cal}}$ at prevalences from $\operatorname{Dir}(\mathbf 1)$, and $b_{\text{cal}}$ minimises over the grid of E7.1 the mean of $\lvert\hat d^{\text{fit}\top}(\hat\pi_s(b)-\pi_s)\rvert$, the part of the accuracy error of the centre that the bandwidth controls; the method is then refitted on the full $V$ at $b_{\text{cal}}$ and compared with the stored method on the protocol of `main`, scored against the realised table of each bag. The theory shows that only the differences between the recalls weight the prevalence error (so a prevalence score would not target accuracy), that the plain accuracy error on calibration bags would let the bandwidth compensate the error of the recalls measured on $V_{\text{fit}}$, which does not carry over to the full $V$, that the maximum-likelihood estimate can stand in for the posterior mean in the criterion (a new hypothesis, checked on the pilot against NUTS), and that a bandwidth fitted on half of $V$ is larger by $2^{1/(K+3)}$, less than one grid step. Only `BayCAP-KDEy-mlp-real` is studied; pilot on `mammographic`, `wine-quality` and `poker_hand`.

The full description is in [ext7_kdey_bandwidth.md](ext7_kdey_bandwidth.md). BayesKDEy and BayesianMAPLS are quantifier classes of their own since WP-25 (`methods/bayes_kdey.py:BayesKDEy`, `methods/bayes_mapls.py:BayesMAPLS`), used by `BayCAPKDEy`, `BayCAPMAPLS` and `SQSB`; `bandwidth="loo"` and the methods, tables and plots of E7.1 are implemented; E7.2 is not. The earlier factorial study of $h$, the surrogate classifier and the quantifier is set aside in [[optional_extensions]] §13.

## Recommended combinations

### Incremental and manageable extension

Evaluate multiple metrics, use the error split of Extension 4 as the frame and its measurements across $(|U|,|V|)$ as the main experiment, add the study of BayCAP (realised-table predictive, Extension 2) with the tempering diagnostic of Extension 5, and verify the main findings with optimized classifiers and quantifiers. This combines Extensions 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7 and is the lowest-risk option, although it remains mainly an empirical contribution unless the results support a new calibration or selection rule.

### SQS-S design extension

Carry out both parts of Extension 6: jointly selecting the simplex geometry, neighbourhood rule, and allocation $\rho$ would turn two sensitivity analyses into a coherent study of how SQS-S uses limited validation data. Extension 4 can then test whether the selected design remains stable as $|U|$ changes, while Extension 7 determines whether it depends on untuned surrogate models.

### Posterior-propagation extension

Add the methods of Extension 3 (BayCAP-KDEy in both pushforwards, SQS-B) and compare them with RQBS and BayCAP on the protocol of `main` and on at least three contingency-table metrics (Extension 1), then measure their error split with Extension 4. Because each method differs from an existing one in one component (the prevalence posterior, the handling of $C$, the source of the prevalences of the validation bags), the comparison isolates which source of uncertainty matters under PPS, which is the setting in which these methods could support a substantive claim rather than a component substitution. Extension 7 should be applied to all competing quantifiers under the same search budget, retaining untuned variants as ablations.

### More ambitious extension

Combine the SQS-S design study (Extension 6) with optimized surrogate models and the posterior-propagation methods of Extension 3, then evaluate the resulting methods across multiple metrics, target sizes, class counts, and domains. The scientific message would become: which representation and propagation of uncertainty remains reliable as the target sample and problem scale change under strict PPS, and are the conclusions robust to model selection?
