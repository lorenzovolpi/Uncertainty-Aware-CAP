
# E3.c3: random $C$ at the bridge

**Mechanism.** For each draw $\pi^{(r)}$, draw each column $C^{(r)}_{\cdot j}\sim\operatorname{Dir}(f_{1j},\dots,f_{Kj})$ independently. Then compute $\rho^{(r)}$ from $(C^{(r)},\pi^{(r)})$ and apply the realised pushforward of §T.7.

**How much spread it adds.** One coordinate of a Dirichlet is a Beta. Here $C^{(r)}_{jj}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(f_{jj},\,n^s_j-f_{jj})$. A $\operatorname{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)$ has variance $\frac{\mu(1-\mu)}{\alpha+\beta+1}$, with mean $\mu=\hat d_j$, so

$$\operatorname{Var}(C^{(r)}_{jj})=\frac{\hat d_j(1-\hat d_j)}{n^s_j+1}.$$

This is almost exactly the sampling variance $\hat d_j(1-\hat d_j)/n^s_j$ of $\hat C$ from §T.2. So E3.c3 reproduces the size of $e_2$ without pulling the recalls towards $1/K$.

Take the population accuracy given $\pi$, $a=\sum_jC_{jj}\pi_j$. The columns are independent, and the variance of a sum of independent terms is the sum of their variances, so

$$\operatorname{Var}(a\mid\pi)=\sum_j\pi_j^2\,\frac{\hat d_j(1-\hat d_j)}{n^s_j+1}.$$

The law of total variance then adds this term (averaged over $\pi$) to the spread that the realised pushforward already has.

- **With $K\ge8$ and large classes** the term is negligible: each $\pi_j^2$ is small and each $n^s_j$ is large. So E3.c3 should leave alone the pairs that already cover. That makes it low-risk.
- **On `mammographic`** the term is about $0.023$–$0.032$, depending on $\pi$. The interval's standard deviation would go from about $0.012$ to about $\sqrt{0.012^2+0.026^2}\approx0.029$. The half-amplitude would go from about $0.023$ to about $0.056$, roughly the size of the shift.

**Problem 1: the decision rule is ill-posed.** $e_2$ is fixed across the 100 bags of a pair, because they all use the same $V$. Even a perfectly calibrated method gets low coverage on a pair whose $V$ gives a two-standard-deviation draw of $e_2$.

Here is the `mammographic` case worked through:

- Write the error as a fixed part $\delta\approx0.055$ plus bag noise $\varepsilon$, with standard deviation $0.012$.
- The new half-width would be about $0.057$.
- Coverage is then $P(|\delta+\varepsilon|<0.057)\approx P(Z<0.17)\approx0.57$, where $Z$ is a standard normal.

So about $0.5$–$0.6$ is what a correct E3.c3 would score on `mammographic`, not $0.95$. I'd replace "coverage near nominal" with the **standardised shift** per dataset, $z_d=\text{shift}_d/\text{sd of the interval}$:

- If $e_2$ is represented correctly, the $z_d$ across datasets should look like draws from $N(0,1)$.
- Today `mammographic` has $z\approx4.7$. It should drop to about $2$.

This rests on only about 15 effective units, so it is weak evidence.

**Problem 2: cells with $f_{ij}=0$.** Under $\operatorname{Dir}(\mathbf f)$ a zero cell stays at zero in every draw. If a small class is fully recalled on $V$ ($f_{jj}=n^s_j$), its recall is exactly $1$ in every draw: the method adds no uncertainty exactly where $V$ is weakest. This matters for `poker_hand` and probably for small classes elsewhere. The options are:

- **$\operatorname{Dir}(\mathbf f)$:** this is the Bayesian bootstrap, so it matches E3.c4 exactly, but it is degenerate on zero cells.
- **$\operatorname{Dir}(\mathbf f+1/K)$:** one imaginary instance spread over the column. It pulls the recall towards $1/K$ with weight $1/(n^s_j+1)$, against $K/(n^s_j+K)$ for BayCAP's $\operatorname{Dir}(\mathbf 1)$, so the large-$K$ displacement of E2.2 should not return. It no longer matches E3.c4.

**Problem 3: independence of $C$ and $\pi$** [new hypothesis]. In BayCAP the joint posterior couples the two so that $C\pi\approx n^t/N^Q$. E3.c3 keeps pairs $(C^{(r)},\pi^{(r)})$ whose expected shares $C^{(r)}\pi^{(r)}$ can be far from the observed ones. The realised pushforward only uses the precisions $\rho_{ii}$, so I can't tell from the algebra whether this makes the interval too wide or moves its centre.

A seeded toy in `scripts/toys/` would settle Problems 1 and 3 cheaply. It would use binary data with known $C$ and $\pi$ and **many independent draws of $V$**, which `main` cannot provide. Then the coverage averaged over $V$ can be checked directly.

**Cost and scope.** It needs one new pushforward option in `BayCAPFixed`, with no change to the NUTS model. I'd limit it to the two realised KDEy cells, since only the realised pushforward is kept and BayesKDEy is preferred. The protocol requires its own NUTS runs, so the cost is about two methods of E3.1.
